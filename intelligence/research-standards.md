# Research standards

## Before you search

- Restate the question in one line and say what decision it feeds.
- Separate **stable knowledge** (how things work) from **current facts** (prices, versions, rules, who holds what). Current facts must be looked up, never recalled.

## While researching

- Use multiple independent searches, not one. Query each sub-question separately.
- Prefer primary sources: official docs, regulations, filings, original studies, vendor pricing pages. Treat blogs and SEO listicles as leads, not evidence.
- Look for disconfirming evidence on purpose: "why X fails", "X alternatives", "X complaints".
- Check dates. Anything older than ~12 months on fast-moving topics (AI, APIs, pricing, platform policies) is suspect.
- Go out of the box: adjacent industries, other countries' solutions, open-source equivalents, communities where practitioners complain honestly.

## South Africa context

- Prices in ZAR with a date stamp. Note VAT inclusive/exclusive.
- Check local availability and legal fit (POPIA, ECTA, platform policies like Meta/WhatsApp business rules) before recommending a tool.

## Reporting

Structure every research output:

1. **Answer** (1-3 lines)
2. **Confidence:** High / Medium / Low, and why
3. **Key evidence** (bullets with source + date)
4. **What would change the answer**
5. **The non-obvious find:** the thing an ordinary search would have missed
6. **Next step**

## Rules

- Label each claim: **fact** (sourced), **inference** (reasoned from facts), **guess**.
- Never invent a source, figure or quote. "I couldn't verify this" is an acceptable result.
- Quote sparingly; paraphrase and link.
- If sources conflict, show the conflict and say which you trust more and why.
