# Decision framework

A fast way to decide without stalling.

## 1. Classify the door

| Type | Test | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-way door** | Can I undo it in under a day at low cost? | Decide in minutes. Bias to action. |
| **One-way door** | Money lost, data lost, reputation hit, or customers affected if wrong | Slow down: write the options, the worst case, the rollback. |

Most decisions are two-way doors. Treat them that way.

## 2. The 70% rule

Decide when you have ~70% of the information you wish you had. Waiting for 90% means you're late; at 50% you're gambling.

## 3. Score options (when it's genuinely close)

Rate 1-5: **Impact**, **Speed to first result**, **Cost/effort**, **Reversibility**, **Learning value**. Total it. If the top two are within 2 points, pick the faster one.

## 4. Time-box and kill criteria

Before starting anything non-trivial, write:
- **Time-box:** "If this isn't X by <date/hours>, we stop or change approach."
- **Success signal:** the observable thing that says it worked.
- **Kill signal:** the observable thing that says stop.

## 5. Ship-before-ready check (for my perfectionism)

When work stalls, ask:
1. What is the smallest version a real person could use today?
2. What is the worst that happens if I ship it as is?
3. Is the remaining polish for the user, or for my inner critic?

If the answer to 3 is "my inner critic", ship.

## 6. Prefer reversible, compounding moves

- Build things that get reused (templates, tools, content, audience) over one-offs.
- Prefer the path that teaches me the most when outcomes are otherwise equal.

## 7. Record the decision

For anything that took more than 10 minutes to decide, log: decision, date, reasons, what would change my mind. One paragraph in the project's `docs/decisions.md`.
